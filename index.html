Scope

Machine learning and fuzzy set theory offer complementary perspectives on learning from complex, uncertain, and gradually structured data. Fuzzy sets give machine-learning systems principled ways to represent vagueness, graded concepts, uncertain labels, and linguistic knowledge. Conversely, machine-learning techniques make it possible to construct, adapt, validate, and compress fuzzy sets and fuzzy systems directly from data.

ML4FS brings together researchers working at this two-way interface. We welcome contributions on methods in which fuzzy representations improve learning, robustness, interpretability, and human–machine interaction. We equally welcome methods that use statistical, kernel-based, evolutionary, neural, and other machine-learning techniques to induce membership functions, fuzzy rules, aggregation operators, and granular representations.

Particular attention will be devoted to the methodological foundations of data-driven fuzzy-set construction:

  • the semantics and identifiability of learned membership degrees,
  • the incorporation of expert knowledge and structural constraints,
  • the evaluation and comparison of induced fuzzy sets,
  • the trade-off between accuracy, interpretability, complexity, and computational efficiency.

The session also welcomes applications showing how fuzzy and machine-learning methods can cooperate. Examples include trustworthy AI, knowledge representation, bioinformatics, anomaly detection, natural-language processing, decision support, and resource-constrained intelligent systems.

Topics

Topics of interest include, but are not limited to:

  • Data-driven induction of fuzzy sets and membership functions
  • Machine learning from fuzzy, imprecise, or partially reliable data
  • Learning with graded, ambiguous, weak, or uncertain labels
  • Fuzzy classification, regression, clustering, and feature learning
  • Neuro-fuzzy and deep fuzzy systems
  • Evolutionary and optimization-based learning of fuzzy systems
  • Learning fuzzy rules, linguistic models, and aggregation operators
  • Granular computing and shadowed-set learning
  • Interpretability and explainability of fuzzy machine-learning models
  • Statistical validation and evaluation of learned fuzzy sets
  • Robustness, reliability, calibration, and uncertainty quantification
  • Complexity reduction and compression of fuzzy models
  • Online, incremental, and evolving fuzzy learning
  • Hybrid knowledge-driven and data-driven learning
  • Applications of fuzzy machine learning in scientific and societal domains

Submission

Papers for ML4FS are submitted through the main IFSA-EUSFLAT 2027 submission system. They follow the same guidelines, formats, and deadlines as regular papers and go through the same peer review. Three types of contribution are accepted:

Full papers

12–15 pages

plus references. Published in Springer Lecture Notes in Computer Science (LNCS).

Communications

≥ 6 pages

Collected in a separate open collection.

Abstracts

≤ 5 pages

Collected in a separate open collection.

Selected contributions will be considered for publication in extended form in journals or edited books.

Submission system: the link and detailed instructions, including how to select the ML4FS special session, will be published on the conference call for papers page and mirrored here as soon as they are available.

At least one author of each accepted paper must register for the conference and present the work in person.
